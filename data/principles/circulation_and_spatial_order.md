# Circulation, Hierarchy, and Spatial Order

## Circulation as the armature of the plan

Circulation is not leftover space between rooms; it is the armature on which the
plan is organised. The path a person takes — from approach, through threshold, into
the sequence of rooms — is itself a designed experience. Promenade architecturale,
Le Corbusier's term, frames movement as the primary way a building is understood:
the design is revealed in time, view by view, as the visitor advances.

Good circulation is legible from the point of entry. A visitor should be able to
read, without signage, where the important spaces are and how to reach them.
Hidden primary stairs, ambiguous entries, and corridors that branch without
hierarchy all force wayfinding effort that a clear plan would make unnecessary.
Where vertical movement matters, exposing the main stair near the entry makes the
section legible and turns circulation into part of the public experience rather
than back-of-house plumbing.

## Hierarchy: making the important spaces read as important

Hierarchy is the deliberate ordering of spaces so the most significant ones are
perceived as significant. The tools are concrete: ceiling height, daylight,
material change, position on the path, and scale relative to neighbours. A
double-height volume flooded with top light reads as primary; a low, side-lit room
reads as secondary. When every room is treated with equal weight, the plan becomes
flat — the jury cannot find the heart of the project because the design has not
told them where it is.

A useful discipline is to name, before drawing, the single most important space and
the experience of arriving at it. Every hierarchy decision then either serves that
arrival or dilutes it.

## Datum, axis, and procession

A *datum* is a line, plane, or volume that gathers otherwise disparate elements
into relationship — a spine corridor, a repeated structural bay, a continuous
ground plane. An *axis* establishes a line of approach and a sense of
anticipation, often terminating in a significant space or view. *Procession* is the
choreographed sequence of compression and release, dark and light, narrow and
wide, that gives movement through the building its rhythm. These devices are how a
plan acquires order beyond mere adjacency.

## Threshold, transparency, and the solid–void balance

The threshold — the moment of passage from outside to inside, or public to private
— is one of architecture's richest opportunities. Deep thresholds (colonnades,
recessed entries, layered screens) slow arrival and signal importance. Transparency
and the balance of solid and void govern how open or protective a space feels: a
glazed face invites and connects; a solid one shelters and directs. The most
resolved facades modulate this balance to match the program behind them rather than
applying one treatment uniformly.
